# Pre-writing Questions, Story, and the Nugget

A paper costs a lot of effort whether it is good or bad. Answer these before drafting.

## Questions

1. **Goal** — what is the goal and why do people care?
2. **Audience** — who will use or build on this?
3. **Hypothesis** — complete "My hypothesis is…". It must be testable; state how you will know it is true or false. This keeps the work out of incremental engineering (hacking toward slightly better numbers).
4. **Obstacle** — what has prevented this so far?
5. **Nugget** — the key insight that makes it doable (below). Most important, hardest to find.
6. **Elevator pitch** — what the paper is about in ≤ 3 sentences.
7. **Teaser** — what one image conveys the core idea?
8. **Prior work** — key works and what each gets wrong; their limitations point forward.
9. **Evaluation** — how will the method be evaluated quantitatively?
10. **Demo** — how do you show the idea works?
11. **Risks** — what could make it fail?
12. **Data** — is everything needed available?

## Story: Goal → Problem → Solution (repeat)

- Define the goal and make the reader want it (explain why it matters).
- Take it away: prior methods fail, a barrier stands in the way (data, math, code).
- Remove the barrier with a clever idea. Then the next barrier appears; repeat.
- This problem-solution rhythm leads the reader from problem to insight in layers.
- One paper tells **one** story (it can have sub-parts). Two big ideas in one paper means readers remember one.

Structure the argument like a talk: few words, progressive, natural order. If there is no talk, explain it to a friend and note what they need to know, in what order, and what they ask. Use that for the introduction.

## The nugget

- The nugget is the **insight**, not the technical contribution. Insight precedes innovation.
- It is how you see the problem differently from everyone before: what turns unsolvable into solvable.
- Common shape: the field works on hard X and hard Y separately; doing X and Y together makes both easier (a "surprise").
- It connects to what people already knew, then flips it, opening a new opportunity.
- State it without technical details; details come later.

Template to test it:

> The field has focused on ___ while ignoring ___. Treating them in a unified way by ___ makes ___ possible, which improves ___.

Gate: if the user cannot state a nugget, propose 2–3 candidates drawn from their notes and results, and ask which is true to their work. Do not fabricate one.
